starting in elder's quorum last sunday, I reflected several times on a thought i've had recently. 

people just say stuff. 

they just say things and don't even know or fully believe what they are saying half the time. 

at least that's how it feels. 

and, of course, i'm guilty of the same. 

i recycle jokes, quotes, quips and phrases, but i was shocked by how often I felt like i was experiencing it in conversation this past week. 

during the lesson on sunday there were several answers to questions that felt so vague and generic that were met with nodding fro and "yes! thank you brother" from the teacher that I thought, 

"am i the only one that doesn't feel like i got anything out of that response?" 

not in a mean way. but in a way that had me wanting more. "explain what you mean by that!"

for example, a question was asked about prayer. I wish i could remember what exactly was posed to the class, but i only remember it was about prayer. 

someone raised their hand to answer the question and said, "for me, the purpose of prayer is to align my will with God's, like it says in the bible dictionary" 

got it. ok, now he didn't say anything wrong or inaccurate. i just don't know what that means? ya know? not like the words, but in action. what does that look like? give me some examples. how has that played out in your life? how have you learned to pray that way? things of that nature. 

before the answer was fully out of this brother's mouth the teacher started to nod and verbally agree, "mhhhmm, yes. exactly!" 

as i saw others nodding in agreement I wanted to stop the class. "wait! what does that look like? am i the only one that struggles with making that bible dictionary definition a reality in my life?"

the lesson moved quickly. i sat quietly unwillingly to interject and risk waking my sleeping maeve. i continued to sit in silence and wondered to myself, "does everyone understand and feel that answer in their life or are we all just saying stuff?"

elder's quorum continued on this vague definition answer type of train for the rest of the hour. 

i left feeling dejected. 

i can't tell if people don't want to share personal experiences or if they haven't experienced them or both. 

probably both. thats the case for me most of the time. 

as i've thought through this dilemma and talked about it a handful of times i feel like there are three ways to improve the responses in that setting: 

1. the teacher needs to be prepared and willing to ask thought provoking questions AND push on people's answers. 

not in an aggressive or corrective way. but in a "tell me more" way. i don't say this to say it is easy. i think it is a skill to be developed, or at the least, a necessary, time-consuming piece of preparing to teach and facilitate discussion. 

2. the people in the class need to know each other. 

every single elder in the room was sitting in their own pew. the lesson was held in the chapel and there was one elder on each side pew from the front row to the back and two to three elders spaced across each middle pew. what could have been a tight 4-5 pew group was spread across 45 pews. i think the lack of proximity in space and relationship (we are a brand new ward) prevents people from sharing personal experiences. 

3. participants need to be willing to come prepared for the lesson. i think this is two-fold. 
    
first, studying or reading through the material can enhance the responsiveness because people are more familiar (even if they just skimmed once) with the conference talk or chapters in scripture. different thoughts and questions can come to mind in the day(s) leading up to the lesson that otherwise would not have by just experiencing the material for the first time (or first time in a long time) the day of. 

second, participants who are actively living life as apprentices to jesus are more likely to have personal experiences to share surrounding the gospel principles and spiritual formations that govern their daily life. then, when asked a question about an experience with prayer or any form of discipleship the response becomes as easy as answering the oft asked get to know you question, "what do you do for work?" 

am i doing these things? no. i was a silent bystander in the spread out crowd that didn't have an answer of my own to offer up. 

i've just been trying to think how i, and others like me, can improve a simple elders quorum lesson and similar situations.
